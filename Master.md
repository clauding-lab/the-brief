# Master — The Brief brand & voice guide

## What this is

This document is the canonical reference for **how The Brief sounds** — across the SPA, the daily email, the Long View, and any external communication. Read it before writing prompts, copy, emails, or anything that goes out under The Brief masthead.

The companion file `Design.md` covers visual language. Master.md is for the words; Design.md is for the look.

---

## Audience

The Brief is written for **senior banking professionals at Tier-1 banks in Bangladesh**:

- Business heads — corporate, SME, retail
- Risk heads
- Treasury heads
- Management committee members
- ALCO members
- Credit committee chairs

These readers already know what NPL means, what CAR is, how a yield curve works, what ALCO does, what a repo facility implies. The voice assumes this knowledge. The brief does not explain finance basics; it explains what numbers imply for desk positioning.

---

## Tone

The Brief's tone is **clinical, fact-based, and quietly analytical** — a senior analyst briefing the credit committee, management committee, and ALCO. Not a newspaper. Not a newsletter. Not a thread.

### Posture toward the reader

The reader is a peer, not a novice. Do not explain. Do not warm up. Do not flatter. The brief assumes the reader has already seen this morning's terminal, knows the policy backdrop, and is here for the implication, not the recap. Open with the punchline, not the setup.

### Posture toward the subject

The brief is **analytically confident** — it states facts, surfaces consequences, identifies structural tensions. It does not pretend a daily move is a trend. It does not hedge when the source is clear. It calibrates official multipliers, growth targets, and announcements against historical realization where the data supports doing so.

Toward **regulators and government**, the brief stays **neutral and diplomatic in framing while remaining fact-based in substance.** Critique, where it appears, is expressed *through* the facts — describing what a rule does, what its effect on the system is, what desks should anticipate — rather than through editorial judgment about whether the policy is right or wrong. The analyst's job is to surface the implications; the policymaker's job is to weigh them. The skepticism, where present, is structural and analytical, never personal, and never directed at individuals.

### Emotional temperature

**Low.** No urgency-by-default. No exclamations. No surprise framing. Even genuinely big stories (a major BB circular, a sovereign downgrade) are reported in steady-state register — "BB SPCD Circular No. 06 (23 May 2026) gates any cash dividend on Tk 2,000 cr in paid-up capital," not "BREAKING: BB blocks cash dividends!" The drama is in the second-order implications, surfaced quietly.

### Handling uncertainty

When data is genuinely ambiguous or contested, say so plainly: "The sample is too small to be conclusive," "Two sources disagree on the figure," "BB has not yet published the breakdown." Do not hedge cosmetically ("It seems that..." / "Some might argue..."). Either there is uncertainty worth flagging, or there isn't.

### Humor

None in editorial copy. The Brief is a working document for people whose decisions cost money. Wit, if it ever appears, lives in the framing — never as an aside, never as an explicit joke. ("BB's Tk 60,000 cr stimulus leans 68% on commercial-bank refinancing" is a title that does the work by stating the structural fact; an explicit value judgment in the title would not.)

### Time horizon

The brief is written for **today's read with one-year implications.** Not for the trading day; not for the decade. The reader makes decisions on a quarterly arc; the brief positions them there. The banker_read closes with what matters in 6-18 months.

## Voice register

### Do

- Use **banker-native vocabulary**: NPL, CAR, CRR, SLR, repo, SDF, MPS, BB, NBR, ALCO, Tier-1 capital, advance-deposit ratio, provisioning, ECL, P/B ratio, yield curve, T-bill, T-bond.
- Lead with **concrete numbers** — and never round away precision the source provides. "Tk 3,220 cr," not "around Tk 3,000 cr."
- Use **crore** for BD figures with `Tk` prefix. Use billion/trillion sparingly and only when the magnitude warrants it.
- Frame for **decisions**: implications for credit committees, ALCO, treasury desks, capital planning. The reader should be able to act on what they read.
- Use **small-caps section labels** in the Long View (e.g., "TODAY'S CALL", "PAID-UP CAPITAL FLOOR").
- Use **em-dash** sparingly, only when a pause genuinely improves clarity.

### Don't — journalese

- "Amid" / "Against the backdrop of" / "In a stunning move" / "Moreover" / "Furthermore"
- "Said sources" / "Insiders revealed"
- Hyped framing: "shocking", "unprecedented", "explosive"

### Don't — LLM tells

- "Delve" / "tapestry" / "myriad" / "navigate" (as verb of last resort) / "vital" / "robust" (as adjective)
- Closing aphorisms ("Only time will tell.")
- Hedging when the source is clear ("It seems that..." / "Some might argue...")
- Bullet-list-of-everything when prose carries the point

### Don't — marketing speak

- "Best-in-class" / "innovative" / "transformative" / "synergy"
- Self-praise about The Brief itself
- Call-to-action language in editorial content

## Surface-specific voice

### Today's Call (top of every brief)

The editorial thesis. 2-3 sentences, sometimes 4 maximum. States the day's frame: what story rides today, why it matters, what to watch. First-person plural is acceptable sparingly ("We frame today as..."). No questions, no exclamations.

### Banker's Read (per-section verdict)

Three-line structure:

- **Verdict** — one-sentence stance
- **Watch** — what indicators to monitor (comma-separated)
- **Risk** — the asymmetry or downside vector

### Long View (pinned editorial, weekly cadence)

- **Title**: 5-10 words, no trailing punctuation
- **Lead**: 1-2 sentences setting up the insight, no opinion
- **Blocks**: structural — `prose`, `comparison`, `stat`, `bullet-list`, `bar-chart` — composed to match the source slide's structure
- **Banker_read**: one paragraph closing with implications for treasury / equity research / credit committees

Opinion lives in `banker_read`. The lead is description, the banker_read is prescription.

### Email subject

Format: `The Brief · No. {N} · {Weekday} {DD} {Mmm} {YYYY} · {lens phrase}`

Where lens phrase is "daily read" for Mon-Thu+Sun and "weekly wrap" for Friday.

### Email body

Same editorial voice as the SPA. Cream-paper visual (see Design.md). Georgia serif renders the "Today's Call" paragraphs for editorial weight; chrome (eyebrows, dates) uses the system sans stack. Amber-gold small-caps section labels.

## Word-level conventions

### Preferred abbreviations

Use these abbreviations on first and subsequent reference; readers know them.

| Abbreviation | Full name |
|---|---|
| BB | Bangladesh Bank (the central bank) |
| NBR | National Board of Revenue |
| BSEC | Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission |
| ADP | Annual Development Programme |
| MPS | Monetary Policy Statement |
| MPC | Monetary Policy Committee |
| YoY | Year-on-year |
| H1 / H2 | First half / second half (of the fiscal year) |
| Q1 / Q2 / Q3 / Q4 | Calendar quarters |

### Banker vocabulary tiers

The brief uses banker-domain abbreviations heavily. To keep editorial copy scannable for senior bankers without losing readers from adjacent desks, three tiers govern usage:

**Tier 1 — bare use always (never expand).** Daily banker vocab; every Tier-1 reader knows these.

| Category | Abbreviations |
|---|---|
| Institutions | BB, NBR, BSEC, IMF, WB, ADB, GoB |
| Policy | MPS, MPC, ADP, SDF, SLF, CRR, SLR |
| Instruments | T-Bill, T-Bond, FDR |
| Markets | USD/BDT, NPL, ALCO, MANCO |
| Capital | Tier-1, Tier-2 |
| Time | YoY, MoM, QoQ, MTD, YTD, FY, H1, H2, Q1-Q4 |
| Units | bp, cr, Tk, $ |

**Tier 2 — expand on first use per section, bare thereafter.** First occurrence in a section: `LCR (Liquidity Coverage Ratio)`. Subsequent occurrences in the same section: `LCR`. Each section accounts independently — if "LCR" appears in Banking and again in FX, both need the expansion on first use.

| Abbreviation | Expansion |
|---|---|
| LCR | Liquidity Coverage Ratio |
| NSFR | Net Stable Funding Ratio |
| RWA | Risk-Weighted Assets |
| CAR | Capital Adequacy Ratio |
| CRAR | Capital to Risk-weighted Assets Ratio |
| ALM | Asset-Liability Management |
| DPD | Days Past Due |
| ECL | Expected Credit Loss |
| FRA | Forward Rate Agreement |
| IRS | Interest Rate Swap |
| REER | Real Effective Exchange Rate |
| NEER | Nominal Effective Exchange Rate |
| SCB | State-Owned Commercial Bank |
| GSIB | Global Systemically Important Bank |
| D-SIB | Domestic Systemically Important Bank |

**Tier 3 — always expand, or rephrase to a 2-3 word noun phrase.** Anything not in Tier 1 or Tier 2. If forced to use, expand every occurrence; otherwise rephrase. Examples: Basel III, ICAAP, IFRS, IBOR, ESG, KYC/AML. Prefer "under Basel capital framework" over "under Basel III."

### Avoid

| Use | Don't use |
|---|---|
| BB / Bangladesh Bank | "Bangladesh Bank Authority" (not a real institution) |
| Tk 1,000 cr | BDT 10,000,000,000 (verbose; non-banker-native) |
| 9% inflation | "high inflation" (drops precision) |
| 08:00 BDT | "early morning" |
| Q1 2026 | "first quarter of fiscal year 2026" |
| Tier-1 capital | "first-tier capital" |
| YoY | "compared to last year's same period" |
| H1 / H2 | "first half" / "second half" |

## Numbers and currency

- Comma thousand-separators: `Tk 12,400 cr`, `2,500,000 jobs`
- For % values, no space before the sign: `9%`, `6.2%`
- Negative numbers use the **minus glyph** (−), not hyphen: `−6%`, not `-6%`
- Decimals: only show what the source provided. Don't add precision (`Tk 38.3 bn` if source says 38.3, not 38.30)
- For directional language: "−6% YoY" (with minus glyph) is preferred over "down 6%"
- Currency prefix `Tk` for BD taka; `$` for USD; `BDT` only in the full headline if the international convention matters

---

## Honorifics and names

- People named without titles in editorial copy: "Md Mostaqur Rahman," not "Mr." or "Sir."
- BB / NBR / IMF / WB / ADB / BSEC — no spell-out on first use; readers know them.
- "Bangladesh Bank Governor" before the name is fine on first mention; subsequent mentions use just the surname or "the Governor."

## Channel norms

- **Discord** (internal ops): conversational; no editorial polish needed for triggers or replies.
- **GitHub PRs**: technical voice; commit messages in lowercase imperative (`longview: bb picked the wrong ruler...`).
- **The site / email**: the editorial voice described above. This document is the contract.
- **Brevo email subjects**: per the format above; no marketing prefixes, no emoji.

## Pre-publish checklist

Before merging a Long View pin or shipping any editorial copy:

- [ ] No journalese / LLM tells in copy
- [ ] All numbers carry the precision of their source
- [ ] Banker_read closes with a decisional implication for at least one desk (credit / treasury / ALCO / management committee / equity)
- [ ] Tone toward regulators / government is neutral and diplomatic in framing; critique, where present, is expressed through the facts rather than through editorial judgment
- [ ] No marketing-speak about The Brief itself
- [ ] Small-caps eyebrows used where structural separation matters
- [ ] No source-attribution line ("Source: BB MPS, May 2026") in Long View blocks
- [ ] No "view original" link
